Open Awareness

Choiceless Awareness

Mindfulness

How to

Rather than anchoring to one object like the breath, let attention rest open to whatever arises — sounds, thoughts, sensations — noticing each without following it or pushing it away.

Benefits

  • Builds a more flexible, less effortful attention
  • Useful once a stable focus has already been developed
  • Reveals the mind's habitual patterns of grasping and avoiding

Practice

Best attempted after some comfort with Breath Awareness — 15-20 minutes, letting the field of attention stay wide rather than narrowing back to one point.
